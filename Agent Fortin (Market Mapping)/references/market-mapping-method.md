# Market Mapping: Method, Worked Example & Further Reading

Condensed from Pierre-André Fortin's (Anara) published article and training page, SocialTalent's
market-mapping/talent-mapping glossary distinction, and the executive-search "name generation"
tradition (Intellerati / The Good Search). Full provenance and source-quality notes in
`README.md`. This is the reference `SKILL.md`'s phases draw on.

## Fortin's four-phase method (Anara)

Source: [Market Mapping : Révéler les Talents Invisibles](https://anara.fr/market-mapping-reveler-les-talents-invisibles/)

1. **Investigation** — understand the sector's environment, tools, and methodologies before
   naming a company. The tool/platform a sector runs on is often a better anchor than a guessed
   competitor list (see the Veeva worked example below).
2. **Cartographie** — identify the key companies and their ecosystem, not just the two or three
   obvious competitors. This is where the **"Fifth Element" technique** applies: given four known
   companies, find a fifth through what connects them (shared vendor, shared client, shared
   investor, industry-association membership, conference sponsor list). Repeat outward from each
   new find.
3. **Flow Analysis** — understand typical career paths and talent movement between the mapped
   companies. A static company list isn't the deliverable; knowing which companies feed which, and
   in which direction, is.
4. **Targeted Sourcing** — approach identified profiles with the contextual understanding built up
   in phases 1–3, rather than a cold, context-free outreach.

**Iteration principle**: each discovery in Cartographie should refine the next pass — this is
explicitly iterative, not a single linear pass. **Set boundaries**: an unbounded, ever-expanding
company list is treated as a failure mode (analysis paralysis) in Fortin's own material, not
thoroughness — decide what "enough" looks like (a target count, or a saturation signal) before
starting.

## Worked example: Veeva / pharma CRM (Fortin's own case study)

- **Traditional keyword approach**: search "CRM" + pharma sector + "Veeva" directly → ~30 profiles.
- **Market Mapping approach**:
  1. Identify companies that are Veeva's actual clients (Veeva's own case-studies/client-logo
     pages, industry press).
  2. Extract the organization names from that vendor client list.
  3. Build a query/search embedding all of those target companies (not "Veeva" as a keyword, but
     the client companies themselves).
  4. Retrieve CRM/omnichannel profiles specifically from those identified firms.
  5. Deduplicate via spreadsheet.
- **Result**: 138 profiles — over 4x the keyword-only pool.

**Why this generalizes**: any sector with a dominant named tool, certification body, or platform
has an equivalent "vendor client list" to mine — the tool/platform's own public roster of who uses
it is frequently a faster, more complete company list than reasoning about competitors from memory.

## Fortin's training curriculum (for Diane's own further reading)

Source: [Formation Market Mapping — Cartographier un marché](https://anara.fr/formation-market-mapping/)

1. Keyword-sourcing limitations — why boolean alone exhausts the same pools; "the problem isn't
   the tool, it's the question posed."
2. Data collection & activation — gathering market data from public sources/networks/digital
   traces, and qualifying it without direct outreach yet.
3. Market cartography — live construction from a real brief: segmentation, talent pools, observed
   movements, mobility hypotheses (= Flow Analysis above, at deliverable depth).
4. Talent Intelligence & reporting — converting the cartography into a decision-ready deliverable
   for executives/hiring managers, not a raw list.

## SocialTalent's market-vs-talent-mapping distinction

Source: [SocialTalent glossary — Market Mapping](https://www.socialtalent.com/glossary/market-mapping) /
[Talent Mapping](https://www.socialtalent.com/glossary/talent-mapping)

The fork this skill's Phase D is built on: **"market mapping gives aggregate insight; talent
mapping gives named candidates."** Market mapping answers *"is this market viable for our hiring
plan?"*; talent mapping answers *"who exactly should we be talking to?"* — the former informs
workforce/hiring strategy, the latter is the direct input to a sourcing campaign.

**Market mapping's four dimensions** (use these as the structure for a market-picture deliverable):

1. **Supply** — roughly how many professionals match the target profile (LinkedIn data, census/
   industry-report figures).
2. **Demand** — who else is actively hiring this profile right now (job postings, headcount growth,
   expansion announcements).
3. **Compensation** — salary ranges from data providers, candidate-disclosed figures during
   screens, published benchmarks — specific to level/location/role, not a generic average.
4. **Competitive dynamics** — who's the dominant employer, who's growing/shrinking, who exports
   talent into the market vs. who imports it.

## Executive-search "name generation" (alternate/complementary Cartographie technique)

Source: [Intellerati — Executive Talent Mapping Research](https://intellerati.com/executive-search-research/talent-mapping-research/)

The older retained-search research tradition, useful specifically **when target companies are
already named** (e.g. from a JD) rather than when the company list itself still needs building:
researchers identify and profile every relevant executive/employee at a named target company,
verify reporting relationships from public sources (press releases, funding/SEC-style filings,
company "team" pages), and infer the rest from title levels, division names, and department
patterns where no public confirmation exists. This is a "go deep on a short known list" technique
— pair it with the Fifth Element technique (which goes "wide" to find the list in the first place)
rather than treating either as a full substitute for the other.

## Realistic expectations

- **A market map goes stale.** Company lists, org structures, and especially mobility signals
  (layoffs, funding, leadership changes) are time-bound — treat a mapping output as accurate as of
  when it was built, not as a permanent reference, and say so if it's being reused after any real
  gap.
- **Don't let Cartographie run unbounded.** Named explicitly above and worth repeating: an
  ever-growing list is a failure mode in Fortin's own framing, not diligence.
- **The four-dimension market picture is only as good as the weakest data source.**
  Compensation data in particular is frequently thin or self-reported — say so rather than
  presenting a compensation range with false precision.
